# ADR-011: Device services as one family per platform (Abstract Factory)

**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Status:** accepted

## Context

The app runs on a phone (Expo Go) and, in a limited form, in a web browser. Each device service had to know which one it was on: `ExpoCoverCamera` refused in a browser, `Haptics` switched itself off there, and `IsbnCamera` swapped the camera for a message, each with its own `Platform.OS === "web"` check. Each class mixed its phone behavior with its browser fallback, and nothing guaranteed that the services in use belonged to the same platform. `Haptics` was also an all-static class called directly from three components, so its use was invisible to the view model and untested.

## Decision

The view model's device services are created by one abstract factory, `DeviceFamily` (`presentation/model/devices.ts`), with `createCoverCamera()` and `createFeedback()`. `PhoneDevices` makes the real camera (`ExpoCoverCamera`) and haptics (`HapticFeedback`); `BrowserDevices` makes a camera that says to use the phone and silent feedback. `App.tsx` picks the family once, and `LibraryAppModel` takes both services from it. The model gives feedback in the actions that change a tab or switch or show a message, and exposes `cameraAvailable` for the Scan screen. The device store is not part of the family: it belongs to the Data tier and stays in `CompositionRoot`. The other four creational patterns were checked and rejected in `docs/pattern-map.md`.

## Consequences

The "phone or browser?" decision lives in one place, and a phone service can't be mixed with a browser one. Each device class does one job, and feedback is tested through a fake family (`tests/devices.test.ts`). The cost is two small interfaces and two factory classes for two platforms; if the web build is ever dropped, `BrowserDevices` and its two products are deleted and nothing else changes.
